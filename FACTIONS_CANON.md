# NOMAD — Faction Canon (the Bible)

**Mandate:** Ian's merge, 2026-10-20 — sodgame's Military Remnants / Survivors
Union / Rust Skulls + the NOMAD-originals he likes: the Lighthouse Collective,
the Tollkeepers, the Drowned, and maybe the Enclave. Names must not be
cliché — world-grounded (a flooded coast, the dead signal called "the Static",
ferries, toll bridges, barrier islands). Covers the whole storyline: island
(easy-mode start) → Outer Banks → Norfolk/Va Beach → D.C. → hardmode
(post-cure: the government moves in, NEW factions enter — the Mercenaries 2 /
Terraria pattern).

**Ruling on Ian's names:** keep all six he named. The non-cliché work lands on
the *new* names — the hardmode government force, the hardmode regionals, and
the epithets that make the old names feel like they belong to *this* coast.

## 2. Easymode Factions

### 2.1 Lighthouse Collective
**One line:** holdouts at the light rebuilding the radio net, one tower at a time.
**Home territory + base:** the lighthouse itself (story anchor, not claimable)
plus relay listening posts along every region. Visual: antenna farms, solar
arrays, hand-painted signal maps, warm desk-lamp interiors — the only faction
whose bases look *lived-in* rather than fortified.
**Controls/wants:** the radio net. They trade intel the way others trade food:
convoy schedules, Static-surge warnings, relay frequencies. Their project is
the beacon — relight it, and the coast can coordinate again.
**Rep flavor:** *earn* by restoring towers, delivering relay parts, running
messages. *Lose* by destroying comms, selling their frequencies to raiders.
Starts friendly-ish; the quest chain carries the relationship.
**Trader:** yes — Kowalski's bench: tech, mods, intel. Unlocked through the
story, not just rep.
**Signature units/vehicles:** signal techs (non-combat), a jury-rigged relay
van (the island's only rolling radio mast).
**Relationships:** ally — Survivors Union (the Union feeds the towers' crews);
rival — Rust Skulls (raiders strip copper from the lines).
**Story phase:** the island's spine (existing quest chain). They follow the
player down the road — every region has a Collective relay to rebuild. The
beacon choice at the lighthouse ending *seeds hardmode*: light it for rescue
and the Authority hears you; keep it dark and something else does (see §6).

### 2.2 Military Remnants ("the Long Chain")
**One line:** what's left of a chain of command, still giving orders nobody
above them is left to countermand.
**Home territory + base:** the east-cape Coast Guard station — "the airbase"
per POIS_FACTIONS_VEHICLES.md: HESCO perimeter, floodlight towers, olive
drab + stencil markings, motor pool with the endgame toys *visible*.
**Controls/wants:** heavy weapons, the island's only real armory, and the
convoy routes. They want *order* — which reads as control.
**Rep flavor:** starts at **−50** (sodgame's number, kept). *Earn* by running
their contracts: convoy escorts, turning in military tech, recovering deserters'
dog tags. *Lose* by raiding their convoys, killing patrols. This is the
grind faction — the quartermaster at **rep ≥75** sells the best shop in the
game, and you start from hostile. The relationship *is* the grind.
**Trader:** yes — the quartermaster (rep-gated, ≥75). Ammo, gun parts,
military armor. The only military-grade supply line.
**Signature units/vehicles:** disciplined patrols (rifle squads, bounding
overwatch — the only AI that fights like soldiers), the 6x6 APC, tech convoys
with box-truck escorts.
**Relationships:** ally — Lighthouse Collective (tenuous; shared radio
discipline, and the Remnants *need* the net); rival — Rust Skulls (open
hatred; the Skulls' founding myth is a convoy they turned).
**Story phase:** island endgame compound → mainland cordon checkpoints on
the OBX and Norfolk legs. They are the wall the player learns to either
climb or go around.

### 2.3 Survivors Union
**One line:** the town that feeds itself — neighbors with a charter, not an army.
**Home territory + base:** town itself — "the town hall" per POIS brief:
garden boxes, rain barrels, solar roofs, market stalls, murals, string lights
at night. Warm light is their territory marker; you can see Union ground by
its glow. Absorbs The Pier's harbor quarter (honest trade at the docks) and
The Refuge's clinic wing (the church infirmary) — see §5.
**Controls/wants:** food, medicine, people. They run the school-as-dormitory
(the recruit pipeline), the caravan depot, the clinic.
**Rep flavor:** starts at **+25** (friendly default). *Earn* by rescues,
medicine deliveries, caravan escorts. *Lose* by theft in town, harming
civilians. The easy faction to like and the hard one to keep liking once
you see what "the charter" costs dissenters.
**Trader:** yes — the medic (rep ≥25): meds, food, produce, basic mods.
**Signature units/vehicles:** caravan guards (shotguns, pickup + flatbed
technical — work trucks, not war rigs), garden crews.
**Relationships:** ally — Lighthouse Collective; rival — Rust Skulls.
**Story phase:** every region. Wherever people cluster, there's a Union
chapter — each one a little more strained than the last, which is the
long arc: the Union's charter frays the further from home you get.

### 2.4 Rust Skulls
**One line:** raiders who turned the toll road into a religion.
**Home territory + base:** the dune fort (~x 500) — POIS brief's "fort":
spike walls (taller, blood-rust red), chained vehicles as barricades, skull
poles, fire barrels, a fighting pit. The loudest base on the island — engines
idling, metal, fire.
**Controls/wants:** the roads. Tolls, ambushes, the war-rig garage. They want
tribute and they want to be *feared* — fear is their logistics.
**Rep flavor:** starts at **−30**. *Earn* by paying tolls without a fight,
winning pit bouts, raiding *with* them (event chain). *Lose* by gate-crashing
tolls, freeing their captives, killing their crews. Note: Skulls rep is the
only one that can be *useful while negative* — a feared toll-dodger gets
different encounters than a hated one.
**Trader:** none — **null by design.** You don't shop with raiders; you pay
tolls, fight pits, or take their caches. Their "economy" is the toll gate
and the black fence at Ocracoke (the Hollow Market fold, §5).
**Signature units/vehicles:** war-rig pickups (up-armored, spiked), pit
champions (brute-class melee), toll crews at every chokepoint.
**Relationships:** rival — Military Remnants (hatred); rival — Survivors
Union; uneasy — Tollkeepers (competing tolls, occasional price wars).
**Story phase:** everywhere there's a road. They're the reason every region
transition has a price — the tax on the whole journey.

### 2.5 The Tollkeepers
**One line:** the bridge is theirs; the crossing is yours for a price.
**Home territory + base:** the Wright Memorial Bridge — the literal gate
between regions. On the beta island they hold the eastern tollgate (the
causeway out); their real seat is the bridge span itself: a fortified
midpoint with the toll plaza, hanging cages for gate-crashers, and Wraith's
booth.
**Controls/wants:** crossings. Bridge tolls, causeway maintenance, the
chokepoint economy. They are neutral the way a turnstile is neutral.
**Rep flavor:** starts at 0. *Earn* by paying tolls, doing bridge-repair
jobs, running their courier contracts. *Lose* by gate-crashing or helping
the Drowned smuggle under the bridge (their one true grudge).
**Trader:** yes — the toll plaza exchange: fuel, vehicle parts, passage
contracts. Prices are the toll schedule; haggling is not a concept they
recognize.
**Signature units/vehicles:** gate crews (pikes + rifles, they fight *around*
the booth, never far from it), a tow rig for clearing wrecks off the span.
**Relationships:** rival — The Drowned (bridge vs. water — the ferry dodges
the toll, and the Tollkeepers know exactly whose boats those are);
everyone else is a customer.
**Story phase:** the hinge of every region transition. Island→OBX and
OBX→mainland both run through a Tollkeeper gate or a Drowned ferry —
the two doors, both priced.

### 2.6 The Drowned
**One line:** the flotilla that never came ashore — they watched the coast
die from the water and decided the water was the answer.
**Home territory + base:** the Pamlico Sound flotilla, anchored off the
west shore; their land foot is the **marina/boatyard** — salvage cranes,
dry-dock scaffolds, hulls on blocks, rope-and-tar aesthetics, gull noise
and diesel. Cpt. Selene Vesper commands from a converted ferry.
**Controls/wants:** boats, salvage, the wrecks. The Graveyard of the
Atlantic is their parish — they mark wrecks with their colors and woe
betide the freelance stripper.
**Rep flavor:** starts at 0. *Earn* by delivering salvage, running ferry
contracts, rescuing stranded boats. *Lose* by stripping their marked
wrecks or siding with the Tollkeepers at a crossing.
**Trader:** yes — the chandlery: boat parts, salvage, fish, charts.
The only source of skiff hulls and outboard parts.
**Signature units/vehicles:** salvage crews (divers, riggers), skiffs and
the ferry *Vesper's Wake*.
**Relationships:** ally — Survivors Union (salvage for food, the honest
trade); rival — The Tollkeepers.
**Story phase:** the water door. Every crossing the Tollkeepers tax, the
Drowned can ferry — for a different price and a different risk. In
hardmode they're the salt-kin of the Boneyard (§3.3).

### 2.7 The Enclave (rumor → canon)
**One line:** the signal nobody answers — pre-Static continuity-of-government
that may or may not still exist.
**Easymode presence:** rumors only. Locked dead-drop caches with tech that
doesn't match any faction's make. A radio burst on a dead frequency, once.
Supply drops nobody claims. *They are a question mark the player finds
evidence of, never an answer.*
**If canonized (see Decision 1):** a hardmode faction — bunker-born,
isolationist, trading tech for tech, answering to a chain of command that
predates the Static. Their hardmode role: the third pole — neither the
Authority's ledger nor the Listeners' Static, but a faction that believes
the old world can simply be *restarted*.
**Trader:** hardmode only — the dead-drop exchange (leave valuables, find
tech). No faces, no haggling.

## 3. Hardmode Factions (post-cure — the new devils)

The cure flips the game. The Static is gone, the dead signal is silent —
and the silence is *valuable*, so everyone comes back to claim it. Same
map, new owners: the Mercenaries 2 pattern.

### 3.1 The Reclamation Authority
**One line:** the government came back with the cure and a ledger — and
everything you built is now an unpermitted structure on federal land.
**Home territory + base:** wherever the old region capitals were. They land
by sea and air: prefab admin compounds, white-and-blue cordon tape made
concrete (checkpoints, curfews, registration tents), the D.C. seat itself.
Their bases look *new* — the only faction building with fresh materials —
which is exactly what's menacing about them.
**Controls/wants:** compliance. Registration of survivors, confiscation of
"unsafe" tech (your modded guns, your radio net), taxation of salvage. They
are not evil; they are *administrative*, which is worse.
**Rep flavor:** starts at 0 and moves fast. *Earn* by compliance contracts:
bounties on Listeners, turning in unregistered tech. *Lose* by harboring
Listeners, raiding Authority depots, running unlicensed convoys. Compliance
is a real path — with real costs (your base gets "permitted," your arsenal
gets smaller).
**Trader:** yes — the licensed quartermaster (compliance-gated): Authority
armor, standardized weapons, permits. The best gear in hardmode, priced in
obedience.
**Signature units/vehicles:** compliance patrols (body armor, non-lethal-
first doctrine — they *arrest* before they shoot, which is its own horror),
MRAP-style carriers, medevac helos (the sky belongs to them now).
**Relationships:** enforces on — everyone non-compliant; betrayed "ally" —
The Boneyard (the fleet requisition, §3.2, is hardmode's first war).
**Story phase:** hardmode only. They arrive with the cure. The easymode
factions don't vanish — they go underground, get permitted, or get raided.

### 3.2 The Boneyard
**One line:** the shipbreakers of the dead fleet — Norfolk's navy yards,
stripped by the people who used to maintain them.
**Home territory + base:** the Norfolk naval yard — crane-city, dry docks,
hulls cut open like tins, torch-light at night. A working industrial town
built inside a dead navy.
**Controls/wants:** the fleet. Ship steel, heavy vehicles, engines the size
of houses. They are the hardmode heavy industry — and the Authority wants
to *requisition* the fleet, which is how the first hardmode war starts.
**Rep flavor:** starts at 0. *Earn* by hauling ship steel, running their
torch crews' supply lines. *Lose* by selling fleet intel to the Authority.
Salt-kin to the Drowned — boat people recognize boat people, and that
recognition is a mechanic (Drowned rep carries over at half value).
**Trader:** yes — the breaker's exchange: heavy vehicle parts, ship steel,
the only source of APC-scale armor plate in hardmode.
**Signature units/vehicles:** torch crews, yard tugs, a working crane (the
set piece), up-armored haulers.
**Relationships:** ally — The Drowned (salt-kin); rival — The Reclamation
Authority (the requisition). Neutral to the easymode factions — they'd
rather sell to everyone than fight anyone, until the Authority forces
the choice.
**Story phase:** introduced in the easymode Norfolk leg as background
(the yards, the torches — a place, not a faction), fully active in
hardmode.

### 3.3 The Listeners
**One line:** the ones trying to tune the Static back in — because to them,
the cure wasn't salvation, it was *silence*.
**Home territory + base:** the Static-scarred zones — dead relay stations,
the lighthouse at night, anywhere the old signal used to live. Their
"base" is a tuned array, not a fort: dishes pointed at nothing, cables
into the ground, people sitting very still, *listening*.
**Controls/wants:** the signal. They tamper with relays, beacons, and —
worst — husks. Post-cure husks are dormant, not gone; the Listeners are
learning to *wake* them.
**Rep flavor:** none. No trader, no negotiation, no rep stat. They are the
horde-adjacent antagonist — the faction you cannot talk to. (The Enclave,
if canonized, is the talkable alternative to their thesis.)
**Trader:** none, by design — like the Skulls' null trader, but for horror
instead of economics.
**Signature units/vehicles:** "tuned" husks (dormant husks re-awakened and
*herded*), static-rigged technicals, the choir (unarmed, chanting, the
most disturbing encounter in hardmode).
**Relationships:** rival — everyone. Even the Skulls find them wrong.
**Story phase:** hardmode only. If the player kept the lighthouse beacon
dark at the ending, the Listeners start stronger (see Decision 4).

## 5. What Happens to the Old src Factions

`src/data/factions.ts` defines six NOMAD factions; the canon keeps three,
folds three. (Phase 3 ticket — the rewrite, not this doc.)

- **Keep as factions:** Lighthouse Collective, The Tollkeepers, The Drowned.
- **The Pier** → folded into the **Survivors Union** as the harbor quarter.
  Cpt. Idris Boudreaux becomes the Union's harbormaster. The honest-trade
  niche survives as the Union's dock market.
- **The Refuge** → folded into the **Survivors Union** as the clinic wing.
  Sister Mariah Okonkwo runs the church infirmary. Medicine stays Union —
  one medic trader, not two.
- **Hollow Market** → folded into **Rust Skulls'** orbit as the Ocracoke
  fence — the black-market outlet the Skulls deny owning. The Accountant
  stays; the faction tag goes.

Net: three in-slice src factions become two kept + three folded locations.
The Remnants/Union/Skulls join as the other three canonical factions.

## 8. Open Decisions for Ian (5, with recommendations)

1. **The Enclave: canonize as a hardmode faction, or keep it a forever-rumor?**
   *Recommend: canonize.* The rumor seeding across easymode pays off as
   hardmode's third pole — neither the Authority's ledger nor the Listeners'
   Static, but the faction that believes the old world can be *restarted*.
   A permanent rumor is a promise you never keep.
2. **Fold The Pier / The Refuge / Hollow Market as proposed (§5)?**
   *Recommend: yes.* Six in-slice factions was already more than the story
   could feed; seven canonical factions (six + Enclave rumor) keeps every
   relationship on screen. The people and places survive — only the faction
   tags go.
3. **Town: rep-gated Union privilege, or claimable home base?**
   *Recommend: rep-gated privilege.* Town is the Union's home — the player
   earns base rights there through rep (a compound plot, not the town), with
   a hostile-takeover quest chain as the Phase 3 alternative for low-rep
   players. BASE_DESIGN's "prize base" survives as the *hardest-earned*
   base, which is better than the easiest-claimed.
4. **Does the lighthouse beacon choice pick the hardmode flavor?**
   *Recommend: yes.* Light it for rescue → the Authority arrives strong,
   Listeners start weak. Keep it dark → the Authority arrives late and
   suspicious, the Listeners start strong. Same roster, different opening
   state — the ending choice becomes load-bearing instead of decorative.
5. **Hardmode traders: who gets one?**
   *Recommend: Authority yes (compliance-gated licensed quartermaster),
   Boneyard yes (the breaker's exchange), Listeners no (non-negotiable by
   design, like the Skulls' null trader).* The Enclave, if canonized, gets
   the dead-drop exchange — no faces, no haggling.

---

*Roster discipline going forward: no new factions without retiring or
folding an old one. Ten is the ceiling — six you can know, one you can
wonder about, three you can fear. The moment an eleventh faction appears,
two of these stop being characters and start being set dressing.*
